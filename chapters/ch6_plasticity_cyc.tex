\framepart{Outline}
Chapter~\ref{ch:pl-num} computed an elastoplastic evolution step by step. In
every step a local problem, the return map at each Gauss point, alternates
with a global problem, equilibrium, until both are satisfied; then the next
step starts. This chapter keeps the two problems and changes the order in
which they are visited. The local stage is applied to a whole history at
each point, the global stage to all the instants of that history, and the
two alternate until the history converges. This is the idea of the
\emph{large time increment} (LATIN) method of
Ladev\`eze~\cite{LadevezeNCSM} and of the \emph{direct methods} of the
\'Ecole polytechnique for moving and cyclic
loads~\cite{DangVanMaitournam1993,MaoucheMaitournamDangVan1997,MaitournamPommierThomas2002}.
They compute the stabilized response of a structure under a cyclic or a
moving load without following the history cycle after cycle, which is what
fatigue design needs.

Two small structures are carried through the whole chapter. The three-bar
truss is a structure with one redundancy, loaded by forces: it shows how
plasticity redistributes the forces between the bars. The Bree vessel is a
wall loaded by a pressure and a cyclic temperature gradient: it shows
ratchetting. Each method of the chapter is computed on both, and the plots
show the computation itself, in time and in space, not only its result.

\paragraph{By the end of this chapter you should be able to:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item write the elastic solution, the residual stresses and the asymptotic
  regimes of the three-bar truss and of the Bree vessel;
  \item split an elastoplastic evolution into admissible and constitutive
  histories, and write the Melan decomposition of the solution;
  \item describe the incremental method and the iteration on the whole history
  as two orders of the same local and global computations, and say what each
  order guarantees;
  \item program the direct cyclic method, recognize its convergence, its
  non-convergence (ratchetting) and its non-unique limits;
  \item state the convergence theorem of the LATIN method, and explain the
  role of the search directions and of the relaxation;
  \item use Zarka's simplified analysis to estimate a shakedown state without
  following the history.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Plan of the chapter.} Section~\ref{sec:cy-models} presents the two
model problems and their asymptotic regimes. Section~\ref{sec:cy-split}
writes the evolution problem on a time window as the intersection of two sets
of histories. Section~\ref{sec:cy-orders} compares the two orders of
computation. Section~\ref{sec:cy-latin} treats the search directions and the
LATIN method, Section~\ref{sec:cy-period} the period map of cyclic loads,
Section~\ref{sec:cy-dcm} the direct cyclic and stationary methods, and
Section~\ref{sec:cy-zarka} Zarka's simplified analysis.
Section~\ref{sec:cy-map} places the other direct methods. A summary,
exercises with solutions (page~\pageref{sec:cy-exo}) and the references close
the chapter.

\begin{gbox}{Guiding questions}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt]
  \item What does a structure become after many cycles of load, and how can
  this state be computed without following the cycles?
  (Sections~\ref{sec:cy-models}, \ref{sec:cy-period})
  \item Which equations are linear and global, which are nonlinear and local,

\section{Two model problems}\label{sec:cy-models}
\index{model problems (cyclic plasticity)}%
Both structures are made of \emph{fibres} in uniaxial stress: the bars of the
truss, the layers of the wall. Each fibre $i$ has a strain $\varepsilon_i$, a
stress $\sigma_i$, a plastic strain $\varepsilon^p_i$ and the law of the
filament of Chapter~\ref{ch:pl-num} with linear kinematic hardening:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cy-fibre}
  \sigma_i=E\,(\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon^p_i-\varepsilon^{th}_i),\qquad
  |\sigma_i-H\varepsilon^p_i|\le\sY,
\end{equation}
with the normality rule of Section~\ref{ssec:pl-1d-kin} and the back stress
$q_i=H\varepsilon^p_i$; $H=0$ is perfect plasticity and $\varepsilon^{th}_i$ a
thermal strain. The strains derive from a few nodal unknowns $\mat{u}$ and
the stresses balance the loads $\mat{F}(t)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cy-discrete}
  \boldsymbol\varepsilon=\mat{B}\,\mat{u},\qquad
  \mat{B}\T\mat{W}\boldsymbol\sigma=\mat{F}(t),
\end{equation}
where $\mat{W}$ is the diagonal matrix of the fibre volumes. This is the
finite element structure of Section~\ref{sec:pn-incr} with one Gauss point per
fibre, small enough to be solved by hand and to be plotted completely.

\subsection{The three-bar truss}\label{ssec:cy-truss}
\index{three-bar truss}\index{residual stress!three-bar truss}%
The truss of Maitournam~\cite[Sec.~3.6]{Maitournam2012c} has three bars of
the same section $S$ and modulus $E$, hinged at the node $O$ and at the fixed
points $A$, $B$, $C$ (Figure~\ref{fig:cy-truss}). Bar~3 has the length $l$
and the direction $\vect e_1$; bars~1 and~2 have the length $l\sqrt2$ and
make angles of $\pm45^\circ$ with it. A force $\vect Q=Q_1\vect e_1+Q_2\vect
e_2$ acts at $O$. The bars carry the normal forces $N_i=S\sigma_i$, and the
elongation of bar $i$ is $\delta_i=\vect t_i\cdot\vect u$, where $\vect t_i$ is
its unit vector oriented towards $O$ and $\vect u$ the displacement of $O$.

\paragraph{Elastic solution.}
Equilibrium of $O$ gives two equations for three forces:
$N_2=N_1-\sqrt2\,Q_2$ and $N_3=Q_1+Q_2-\sqrt2\,N_1$. The truss is redundant of
degree one: the self-equilibrated forces form the line
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cy-truss-self}
  (N_1,N_2,N_3)=\gamma\,(1,1,-\sqrt2),\qquad \gamma\in\R .
\end{equation}
The elongations are compatible if they derive from a displacement of $O$,
that is, if they are orthogonal to the self-stresses:
$\delta_1+\delta_2=\sqrt2\,\delta_3$. With $\delta_i=N_il_i/(ES)$ this gives
$N_1+N_2=N_3$ and the elastic solution~\cite[eq.~(3.46)]{Maitournam2012c}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cy-truss-el}
  N^{el}_1=\frac{Q_1}{2+\sqrt2}+\frac{Q_2}{\sqrt2},\qquad
  N^{el}_2=\frac{Q_1}{2+\sqrt2}-\frac{Q_2}{\sqrt2},\qquad
  N^{el}_3=\frac{2Q_1}{2+\sqrt2}.
\end{equation}
Under $Q_1$ alone, bar~3 yields first, at $Q_1=(2+\sqrt2)N_0/2\approx1.71N_0$
with $N_0=S\sY$, and the truss collapses at the limit load
$Q_L=(1+\sqrt2)N_0\approx2.41N_0$, when bars~1 and~2 yield as well.

\paragraph{Residual forces and the kernel.}
\index{Melan decomposition!three-bar truss}%
Let the bars carry plastic elongations $\delta^p_i=l_i\varepsilon^p_i$ at zero
load. The forces are self-equilibrated, hence of the
form~\eqref{eq:cy-truss-self}, and compatibility of the total elongations
$\delta_i=N_il_i/(ES)+\delta^p_i$ fixes $\gamma$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cy-truss-res}
  \gamma=-\frac{ES}{(4+2\sqrt2)\,l}\,\bigl(\delta^p_1+\delta^p_2-\sqrt2\,\delta^p_3\bigr).
\end{equation}
The residual forces see only the combination
$\delta^p_1+\delta^p_2-\sqrt2\,\delta^p_3$, the incompatibility of the plastic
elongations. A compatible plastic elongation, for instance $(a,-a,0)$, which
is the elongation produced by a vertical displacement of $O$, leaves no
residual force: it moves the node and nothing else.

\paragraph{Three loadings.}
Three periodic loadings of period $T=2\pi$ produce the three asymptotic
regimes of Section~\ref{sec:cy-period} (perfect plasticity,
Exercise~\ref{exo:cy-q-truss}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item $Q_1=1.1N_0(1-\cos t)$, $Q_2=0$: bar~3 yields in the first cycle, then
  the response is elastic. The range of $N^{el}_3$ is $1.29N_0<2N_0$; this is
  \emph{elastic shakedown}.
  \item $Q_1=1.9N_0\sin t$, $Q_2=0$: the range of $N^{el}_3$ is
  $2.23N_0>2N_0$. Bar~3 yields in tension and in compression in every cycle;
  this is \emph{alternating plasticity}.
  \item $Q_2=N_0$ constant and $Q_1=1.2N_0\sin t$: the constant force loads
  bars~1 and~2 with opposite signs, the alternating one with the same sign.
  Bar~1 yields in tension in one half-cycle, bar~2 in compression in the
  other, and the plastic elongations grow by $(a,-a,0)$ in every cycle, with
  $a=0.4\,\delta_Y$ ($\delta_Y=\sY l\sqrt2/E$): this is \emph{ratchetting}.
  The ratchet increment is compatible: it lies in the kernel of the
  residual-stress operator, and nothing in the stresses opposes it.
\end{itemize}
With kinematic hardening $H=0.05E$ the third loading no longer ratchets: the
plastic elongations converge, in about sixty cycles, to a state of elastic
shakedown. This slow approach is the test case of
Section~\ref{sec:cy-orders}.

\subsection{The Bree vessel}\label{ssec:cy-bree}
\index{Bree problem}\index{ratchetting!Bree problem}%
A thin vessel of mean radius $r_m$ and thickness $e$ contains a fluid at a
constant pressure, and its wall is crossed by a heat flux that is switched on
and off: the temperature varies linearly through the wall, with an amplitude
$\tau_0\lambda(t)$, $\lambda(t)=|\sin t|$. This is the problem of
Bree~\cite{Bree1967} for the fuel cans of fast reactors, in the form of the
spherical vessel of Maitournam~\cite[Sec.~4.5]{Maitournam2012c}. The only
stress is the hoop stress $\sigma(x,t)$, a function of the position
$x=(r-r_m)/e\in[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]$ in the wall, and the hoop strain is the
same in all the layers:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cy-bree}
  \sigma(x,t)=\tilde E\,\bigl(\varepsilon(t)-\varepsilon^p(x,t)+\alpha\tau_0x\lambda(t)\bigr),
  \qquad
  \int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\sigma(x,t)\dd x=\sigma_P ,
\end{equation}
where $\tilde E=E/(1-\nu)$ and $\sigma_P=p\,r_m/(2e)$ is the membrane stress
of the pressure. In the notation~\eqref{eq:cy-discrete}, the layers are the
fibres, $\mat{B}$ is a column of ones and $\mat{W}$ the layer thicknesses.

\paragraph{Elastic solution and residual stresses.}
Without plastic strain the mean of~\eqref{eq:cy-bree} gives
$\tilde E\varepsilon=\sigma_P$, hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cy-bree-el}
  \sigma^{el}(x,t)=\sigma_P+2\sigma_T\,x\,\lambda(t),\qquad
  \sigma_T=\tfrac12\tilde E\alpha\tau_0 :
\end{equation}
the outer skin is in tension and the inner skin in compression during
heating. With a plastic strain, the mean of~\eqref{eq:cy-bree} gives
$\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{el}+\langle\varepsilon^p\rangle$, where
$\langle\cdot\rangle$ is the mean through the wall, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cy-bree-melan}
  \sigma=\sigma^{el}+\rho,\qquad
  \rho(x,t)=-\tilde E\,\bigl(\varepsilon^p(x,t)-\langle\varepsilon^p\rangle(t)\bigr).
\end{equation}
The residual stress is the deviation of the plastic strain from its mean,
times $-\tilde E$. The mean itself, a uniform plastic strain, is compatible:
it changes the radius of the vessel and produces no stress. Ratchetting will
be a growth of $\langle\varepsilon^p\rangle$, invisible in the stresses.

\paragraph{The Bree diagram.}
\index{Bree diagram}%
With $X=\sigma_P/\sY$ and $Y=\sigma_T/\sY$, perfect plasticity, the
asymptotic regime depends only on $(X,Y)$ (Figure~\ref{fig:cy-bree-diagram}).
The response is elastic for $X+Y\le1$. It shakes down elastically for
$Y\le2$ and $X+Y/4\le1$, alternates for $Y>2$ and $XY\le1$, and ratchets
otherwise~\cite{Bree1967}; Maitournam~\cite[Sec.~4.5]{Maitournam2012c}
derives the ratchetting boundary $X+Y/4=1$ by following the first three
half-cycles. The figure is computed with the incremental method of
Chapter~\ref{ch:pl-num}, cycle after cycle, on a grid of $50\times60$ load
pairs; the computed regimes follow Bree's lines to the resolution of the
grid. The two cases marked (a) and (b) are used in
Sections~\ref{sec:cy-period}--\ref{sec:cy-dcm}.

\section{Two groups of equations on a time window}\label{sec:cy-split}
\index{global--local method}%
\subsection{Admissible and constitutive histories}
The quasi-static evolution problem of Chapter~\ref{ch:pl} (box in
Section~\ref{ssec:pl-bvp-eq}) splits into two groups of equations
(Figure~\ref{fig:pl-tonti}). Chapter~\ref{ch:pl-num} used the split at one
instant. Here it is used on a \emph{time window} $I$: one step $[t_n,t_\np]$,
the whole loading interval $[0,T]$, or one period. A \emph{history} on $I$ is
a set of fields $s=(\vu,\vz,\vSig)$ on $\Omega\times I$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item The \emph{admissible histories} $\Ad$ satisfy, at every instant of $I$,
  the kinematic conditions, equilibrium and the state laws
  $\sig=\bbC:(\eps[\vu]-\epsp)$, $\vq=-\bbD\valpha$, together with a
  \emph{closure} condition: the initial state $\vz(t_0)=\vz_0$, or the
  periodicity $\vz(t_0)=\vz(t_0+T)$. These equations are linear and global in
  space, and instantaneous in time: $\Ad$ is an affine set.
  \item The \emph{constitutive histories} $\Gcon$ satisfy the flow rule
  $\dot\vz\in N_{\Esig}(\vSig)$ at every point of $\Omega\times I$. These
  equations are nonlinear and local in space: each point carries its own
  evolution in time.
\end{itemize}
The solution is the history that belongs to both: $s\in\Ad\cap\Gcon$. Every
method of this chapter builds alternately a point of $\Ad$ (the \emph{global
stage}) and a point of $\Gcon$ (the \emph{local stage}).

\begin{proposition}[The two signs]\label{prop:cy-signs}
Let $s$, $s'$ be two histories on $I=[t_0,t]$ with the same closure data. If
$s,s'\in\Ad$, then
\[
  \int_{t_0}^{t}\!\!\int_\Omega(\vSig-\vSig')\cdot(\dot\vz-\dot\vz')\dd x\dd\tau
  =-\tfrac12\norm{(\vSig-\vSig')(t)}_E^2\le0
\]
when $\vSig(t_0)=\vSig'(t_0)$. If $s,s'\in\Gcon$, the same integral is
non-negative.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For admissible histories, the computation of the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:pl-unique} gives
$\displaystyle \frac{\dd}{\dd t}\tfrac12\norm{\Delta\vSig}_E^2=-\int_\Omega\Delta\vSig\cdot\Delta\dot\vz\dd x$,
because $\Delta\sig$ is self-equilibrated and $\Delta\dot\vu$ kinematically
admissible with zero data; integrate in time. For constitutive histories the
integrand is non-negative at every point by the monotonicity of the normal
cone (Corollary~\ref{cor:pl-monotone}).
\end{proof}
The uniqueness theorem of Chapter~\ref{ch:pl} is the case where $s$ and $s'$
are in both sets. The convergence proofs of
Section~\ref{sec:cy-latin} play the two signs against each other.

\subsection{The Melan decomposition}
\index{Melan decomposition}\index{residual stress!operator}%
The admissible set has an explicit structure. Let $(\vu^{el},\sig^{el})$ be the
\emph{fictitious elastic} response of the structure to the loads, the
response it would have without plasticity. For a plastic strain field $\epsp$
at zero load, let $\vw$ be the solution of the elastic problem with initial
strain $\epsp$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cy-eigen}
  \vw\in\Kad(\vzero,S_u),\qquad
  \int_\Omega\eps[\vw]:\bbC:\eps[\vv]\dd x=\int_\Omega\epsp:\bbC:\eps[\vv]\dd x
  \quad\forall\vv\in\Kad(\vzero,S_u),
\end{equation}
and define $\Pop\epsp=\eps[\vw]$ and $\Zop\epsp=\bbC:(\epsp-\Pop\epsp)$.
In finite elements, \eqref{eq:cy-eigen} is the system
$\mat{K}\mat{w}=\sum_e\int\mat{B}\T\mat{C}\,\epsp$ with the \emph{elastic}
stiffness, which is factorized once.

\begin{proposition}[Melan decomposition]\label{prop:cy-melan}
Every admissible history is of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cy-melan}
  \eps=\eps^{el}+\Pop\epsp,\qquad
  \sig=\sig^{el}+\vrho,\qquad \vrho=-\Zop\epsp .
\end{equation}
The operator $\Pop$ is the projection onto the compatible strains, orthogonal
for the scalar product $\int_\Omega\vect a:\bbC:\vect b\dd x$. The residual
stress $\vrho$ is self-equilibrated; $\Zop$ is symmetric, positive
semi-definite, and its kernel is the set of compatible strains: a compatible
plastic strain produces no residual stress.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The problem is linear in the data $(\text{loads},\epsp)$; the elastic part
gives $\eps^{el}$, the plastic part gives $\Pop\epsp$ by~\eqref{eq:cy-eigen}.
By~\eqref{eq:cy-eigen}, $\vrho=\bbC:(\Pop\epsp-\epsp)$ satisfies
$\displaystyle \int_\Omega\vrho:\eps[\vv]\dd x=0$ for all admissible $\vv$; it is
self-equilibrated, and $\epsp-\Pop\epsp$ is orthogonal to the compatible strains.
If $\epsp=\eps[\vv]$ is compatible, $\Pop\epsp=\epsp$ and $\vrho=\vzero$.
\end{proof}
For the truss, $\Zop$ is the rank-one map~\eqref{eq:cy-truss-res}; for the
Bree vessel, $\Pop\varepsilon^p=\langle\varepsilon^p\rangle$ and
$\Zop\varepsilon^p=\tilde E(\varepsilon^p-\langle\varepsilon^p\rangle)$
by~\eqref{eq:cy-bree-melan}. In both cases the kernel of $\Zop$, the
compatible plastic strains, is the direction of ratchetting. Substituting the
decomposition~\eqref{eq:cy-melan} in the flow rule eliminates displacements
and equilibrium: the evolution problem becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cy-reduced}
  \dot\vz\in N_{\Esig}\bigl(\sig^{el}(t)-\Zop\epsp,\ -\bbD\valpha\bigr),
  \qquad \text{closure on }\vz ,
\end{equation}
an evolution equation for the internal variables alone, local in time and
non-local in space through $\Zop$. It is a \emph{sweeping process} in the
sense of Moreau~\cite{Moreau1977}: the elastic domain, shifted by the elastic
stress $\sig^{el}(t)$, moves with the load and drags the internal variables.

\subsection{Three choices}
The methods of the chapter differ by three choices, which the rest of the
chapter takes one at a time.
\begin{gbox}{Window, closure, search directions}
\index{search directions}%
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Window} of one alternation of the local and global stages: one
  step (Chapter~\ref{ch:pl-num}), the interval $[0,T]$ (LATIN), one period
  (direct cyclic method), one streamline (stationary methods).
  \item \textbf{Closure}: initial state, periodicity $\vz(t_0)=\vz(t_0+T)$, or
  equality of the states upstream and downstream of a moving load.
  \item \textbf{Search directions}: the linear relations that link the point
  of $\Gcon$ to the previous point of $\Ad$ (local stage) and the next point of
  $\Ad$ to it (global stage): consistent tangent (Newton), elastic stiffness
  (initial strain), or a symmetric operator used in both stages (LATIN).
\end{itemize}
\end{gbox}

\paragraph{A dictionary.}
The literature on these methods uses three notations. Ladev\`eze writes the
material in a ``normal formulation'' with internal variables $X$ and forces
$Y=AX$; Dang Van and Maitournam use internal parameters $a_k$ and forces
$A_k=Z:a_k$; Kondo and Maitournam~\cite{KondoMaitournam2023c} use the
irreversible forces $A=-\rho_0\,\partial\Psi/\partial(\cdot)$.
Table~\ref{tab:cy-dictionary} translates them into the notation of this book.
The only trap is a sign: Ladev\`eze's $Y$ and Dang Van--Maitournam's $A_k$ are
$-\vq$, while Kondo--Maitournam's force of a hardening variable is $\vq$.

\section{Two orders for the same computations}\label{sec:cy-orders}
\index{direct method!order of the loops}\index{incremental method!order of the loops}%
\subsection{Step by step, or the whole history at once}

the same two kinds of computations: a \emph{local} return map
$\vz_n=\mathcal R(\eps_n;\vz_{n-1})$ at each Gauss point and instant, and a
\emph{global} linear solve at each instant. They differ in the order of the
loops over the instants $n$ and over the iterations $k$
(Figure~\ref{fig:cy-orders}).

The incremental method of Chapter~\ref{ch:pl-num} loops \emph{for $n$, for
$k$}: it converges the equilibrium of $t_n$ before it looks at $t_\np$. The
iteration on the whole history loops \emph{for $k$, for $n$}: it visits all
the instants with the same (wrong) data, and corrects them all at the next
iteration. With the elastic stiffness in the global stage, the two
algorithms are written below; Box~6.1 is the modified
Newton method of Box~5.3.

\begin{gbox}{Box 6.1\quad Initial-strain iteration, step by step}
\index{initial-strain method}%
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Step.} For $n=1,\dots,N$, start from $\mat{u}^{(0)}=\mat{u}_{n-1}$.
  \item \textbf{Local stage.} At each Gauss point,
  $(\sig^{(k)},\vz^{(k)})=\mathcal R(\mat{B}\mat{u}^{(k)};\vz_{n-1})$.
  \item \textbf{Global stage.} Solve
  $\mat{K}\,\delta\mat{u}=\mat{F}^{\mathrm{ext}}_n-\sum_e\int\mat{B}\T\sig^{(k)}$
  with the elastic stiffness $\mat{K}$, factorized once;
  $\mat{u}^{(k+1)}=\mat{u}^{(k)}+\delta\mat{u}$.
  \item \textbf{Test.} Repeat 2--3 until the residual is small; store
  $\vz_n=\vz^{(k)}$ and go to the next step.
\end{enumerate}
\end{gbox}

\begin{gbox}{Box 6.2\quad Iteration on the whole history}
\index{direct cyclic method!Box 6.2}%
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Initialization.} $\epsp(t_n)=\vzero$ for all $n$ (or a guess);
  closure state $\vz(t_0)$.
  \item \textbf{Global stage.} For every instant $t_n$, solve the elastic problem
  with initial strain: $\mat{K}\mat{u}_n=\mat{F}^{\mathrm{ext}}_n+\sum_e\int\mat{B}\T\mat{C}\,\epsp(t_n)$
  (one factorization, $N$ independent solutions); $\eps_n=\mat{B}\mat{u}_n$.
  \item \textbf{Local stage.} At each Gauss point, sweep the history:
  $\vz(t_n)=\mathcal R(\eps_n;\vz(t_{n-1}))$, $n=1,\dots,N$.
  \item \textbf{Closure.} Initial condition: $\vz(t_0)=\vz_0$. Periodic
  (direct cyclic method): $\vz(t_0)\gets\vz(t_N)$.
  \item \textbf{Test.} Repeat 2--4 until the stresses of the two stages agree
  at all instants (and, if periodic, $\vz(t_N)=\vz(t_0)$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{gbox}

\subsection{What each order guarantees}
\paragraph{Why time stepping is the standard.}
The evolution problem~\eqref{eq:cy-reduced} with an initial condition is a
Cauchy problem, and it is causal: the state at $t$ depends on the past only.
The implicit Euler scheme for a sweeping process, Moreau's \emph{catching-up
algorithm}~\cite{Moreau1977}, is the return map; it is at the same time the
proof of existence of the solution and the algorithm that computes it. Each
step is the minimization of a strictly convex incremental potential
(Theorem~\ref{thm:pn-W}), with a unique solution and a globally convergent
Newton method; the next step starts from a converged state, close to the
solution; and the return map is contractive
(Theorem~\ref{thm:pn-contract}), so that an error made in a step is not
amplified by the following ones. Consistency and stability give the
convergence of the time discretization. Everything is local in time, and so
are the guarantees.

\paragraph{What the whole-history iteration risks.}
\index{ratchetting!non-convergence of direct methods}%
The iteration of Box~6.2 is a fixed-point iteration on
histories, $\epsp\gets\mathcal R_{[t_0,t_N]}(\eps^{el}+\Pop\epsp)$, and the
properties of each step do not transfer to it.
\begin{itemize}
  \item No theorem covers the pair ``return map at fixed total strain, elastic
  stiffness'' on a window. The LATIN theorem of Section~\ref{sec:cy-latin}
  needs other directions and a relaxation.
  \item Rate-independent plasticity has no fading memory. The return map is
  only non-expansive with respect to $\vz_{n-1}$, so that an error made at
  $t_n$ is carried unchanged to all later instants by the local sweep.
  Figure~\ref{fig:cy-truss-lattice}b shows it: a correction made at the first
  plastic instant reappears at every later instant, iteration after
  iteration, while the incremental method confines its iterations to the
  plastic steps.
  \item With the periodic closure, an error that is constant in time is not
  damped by the time coupling at all. In plasticity this is the kernel of
  $\Zop$: a compatible plastic strain added to a periodic state gives another
  periodic state. The iteration may converge to a periodic state that depends
  on its starting point, or not converge at all if the structure ratchets
  (Sections~\ref{sec:cy-period}--\ref{sec:cy-dcm}).
  \item The iterates are not states of any loading history; there is no energy
  that decreases from one iteration to the next unless the directions are
  chosen for it, as in LATIN.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{When it pays.}
The whole-history iteration wins when its strengths matter more than its
risks:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Confined plasticity.} By~\eqref{eq:cy-melan}, the elastic
  prediction is exact outside the plastic zone, and the correction couples
  the plastic points only through $\Zop$ restricted to the plastic zone. A
  small plastic zone in an elastic structure (contact, notches, the skin of a
  thermal shock) gives a fast contraction.
  \item \emph{One loop instead of two.} Cycle after cycle, the incremental
  method converges the equilibrium of every step of every transient cycle,
  which is then thrown away. The direct cyclic method converges the
  equilibrium and the periodicity together.
  \item \emph{Cheap iterations.} The global stage uses the elastic stiffness,
  factorized once; the instants are independent and can be compressed in a
  Fourier or wavelet basis in time~\cite{MaitournamPommierThomas2002}; the
  local stage is parallel in space.
\end{itemize}
Figure~\ref{fig:cy-truss-process} compares the costs on the truss. For three
bars, factorizing the tangent is free, and the cycle-by-cycle computation
needs fewer local evaluations than the direct cyclic method (3750 against
13\,200 to reach $10^{-6}\varepsilon_Y$); Anderson acceleration of the period
map (Section~\ref{sec:cy-period}) needs four cycles. In a finite element model
the balance changes: the incremental method refactorizes the tangent at
every Newton iteration, the direct cyclic method never does, and Maouche et
al.~\cite{MaoucheMaitournamDangVan1997} report a computation of fretting
contact ten times faster in gross slip (3\,h against 30\,h for 74 cycles) and
twice as fast in partial slip.

\section{Search directions and the LATIN method}\label{sec:cy-latin}
\index{LATIN method}\index{search directions}%
\subsection{One step, two directions}\label{ssec:cy-dir}
Ladev\`eze~\cite[Sec.~3.3]{LadevezeNCSM} describes the classical solvers of
one step by two \emph{search directions}. For a fibre loaded by a force, the
admissible points are on the horizontal line $\sigma=\Sigma$ of the plane
$(\varepsilon,\sigma)$ and the constitutive points on the curve $\Gcon$ of the
material (Figure~\ref{fig:cy-directions}). From an admissible point $s_k$,
the \emph{local stage} finds the point $\hat s\in\Gcon$ reached along a
direction $E^+$; from $\hat s$, the \emph{global stage} finds the admissible
point $s_{k+1}$ reached along a direction $E^-$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cy-directions}
  E^+:\ \hat\sigma-\sigma_k=-L^+(\hat\varepsilon-\varepsilon_k),\qquad
  E^-:\ \sigma_{k+1}-\hat\sigma=L^-(\varepsilon_{k+1}-\hat\varepsilon).
\end{equation}
The local direction must be local in space, the global one linear. The return
map of Chapter~\ref{ch:pl-num} is the local stage with the ``vertical''
direction $L^+=\infty$: the total strain is fixed, $\hat\varepsilon=\varepsilon_k$,
and only the stress is corrected. The choice of $L^-$ names the method: the
consistent tangent $E^{\mathrm{alg}}$ gives Newton's method (Box~5.3), the
elastic modulus $E$ gives the initial-strain method (Box~6.1).

\begin{proposition}[Initial strain on the filament]\label{prop:cy-factor}
For a fibre with linear kinematic hardening $H$, loaded by a force on its
plastic branch, the initial-strain iteration multiplies the error on the
strain by
\[
  \kappa=1-\frac{E_t}{E}=\frac{E}{E+H},\qquad E_t=\frac{EH}{E+H}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On the plastic branch $\sigma(\varepsilon)$ is affine with slope $E_t$. The
iteration is $\varepsilon_{k+1}=\varepsilon_k+(\Sigma-\sigma(\varepsilon_k))/E$,
so that $\varepsilon_{k+1}-\varepsilon^\ast=(1-E_t/E)(\varepsilon_k-\varepsilon^\ast)$.
\end{proof}
Without hardening $\kappa=1$: a single fibre loaded by a force has no solution
beyond the yield force. In a structure, the elastic parts that confine the
plastic zone give the contraction: for the truss of Figure~\ref{fig:cy-truss}
with bar~3 plastic, the factor is the stiffness that bar~3 loses at yield,
relative to the stiffness of the truss seen from bar~3
(Exercise~\ref{exo:cy-q-factor}). Nguyen~\cite{Nguyen1977} proved that the
implicit initial-strain iteration of one step converges for every increment
when the hardening is strictly positive; Dang Van and
Maitournam~\cite[Rem.~4.1]{DangVanMaitournam1993} use this result for their
stationary method (Section~\ref{sec:cy-dcm}).

\begin{theorem}[Conjugate directions~{\cite[Sec.~3.3.6]{LadevezeNCSM}}]\label{thm:cy-conjugate}
Let the constitutive relation $\sigma=\mathcal K(\varepsilon)$ of one step be
strictly monotone, and take conjugate directions $L^+=L\T$, $L^-=L$ with $L$
linear, symmetric and positive definite. Then the distance from the admissible
iterates $s_k$ to $\Gcon$, measured by
$d^2(s,\Gcon)=\int_\Omega(\sigma^+-\sigma^-):(\varepsilon^+-\varepsilon^-)\dd x$
with $s^\pm\in\Gcon$ the points reached from $s$ along $E^\pm$, tends to zero.
\end{theorem}
The proof writes a quantity built from the error in the norm of $L$, shows
that it decreases at each iteration by $d^2(s_{k+1},\Gcon)$, and sums: the
two signs of Proposition~\ref{prop:cy-signs} make every term of the sum
non-negative. The pairs ``vertical, tangent'' and ``vertical, elastic'' are
not conjugate, and the theorem does not cover them: Newton's method needs its
own local analysis~\cite{SauterWieners2011}, and the initial-strain method the
hardening of Nguyen's theorem.

\subsection{The large time increment method}\label{ssec:cy-latin}
\index{LATIN method!algorithm}%
The LATIN method applies the same construction to the whole interval
$[0,T]$~\cite{LadevezeNCSM,BoisseBussyLadeveze1990}. The material is written in
a \emph{normal formulation}, where the state law of the hardening variables is
linear with a constant symmetric positive operator; for the linear hardening of
this book it is the law $\vq=-\bbD\valpha$ itself (Table~\ref{tab:cy-dictionary}).
The directions are written on the \emph{rates} of the plastic strain:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cy-latin-dir}
  E^+:\ \dot{\hat\varepsilon}{}^p-\dot\varepsilon^p_k=-h\,(\hat\sigma-\sigma_k),
  \qquad
  E^-:\ \dot\varepsilon^p_{k+1}-\dot{\hat\varepsilon}{}^p=h\,(\sigma_{k+1}-\hat\sigma),
\end{equation}
with a scalar or an operator $h>0$, local in space, the same in both stages.
The local stage is a return map at each point and each instant, with the
stiffness $1/h$ in place of $E$. The global stage is linear but no longer
instantaneous: the plastic strain at $t_n$ depends on its rates at all earlier
instants, so that the admissible problem is a viscoelastic problem of Maxwell
type, solved by marching in time with the modulus $E/(1+Eh)$.

\begin{gbox}{Box 6.3\quad LATIN iteration on $[0,T]$}
\index{LATIN method!Box 6.3}%
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Initialization.} Elastic solution at every instant:
  $s_0=(\sig^{el}(t),\ \epsp=\vzero)$.
  \item \textbf{Local stage.} At each point, for $n=1,\dots,N$: return map
  with stiffness $1/h$ from the trial stress
  $\sigma_k(t_n)+\Delta\varepsilon^p_k(t_n)/h$ and the stored
  $\hat\varepsilon^p(t_{n-1})$; it gives $(\hat\sigma,\hat\varepsilon^p)(t_n)$.
  \item \textbf{Global stage.} For $n=1,\dots,N$: solve the elastic problem with
  the modulus $E/(1+Eh)$ and the initial strain
  $\varepsilon^p(t_{n-1})+\Delta\hat\varepsilon^p(t_n)-h\hat\sigma(t_n)$; update
  $\varepsilon^p(t_n)=\varepsilon^p(t_{n-1})+\Delta\hat\varepsilon^p(t_n)+h(\sigma-\hat\sigma)(t_n)$.
  \item \textbf{Relaxation.} $s_{k+1}=(1-\mu)\,\bar s_{k+1}+\mu\,s_k$, with
  $\bar s_{k+1}$ the result of the global stage and $0<\mu<1$.
  \item \textbf{Indicator.} Stop when the distance between $s_{k+1}$ and
  $\hat s$ in the energy norm is small.
\end{enumerate}
\end{gbox}

\begin{theorem}[Ladev\`eze~{\cite[Sec.~4.5]{LadevezeNCSM}}]\label{thm:cy-latin}
Let the flow rule of the normal formulation be maximal monotone, the
hardening operator $A=\bbD$ symmetric positive definite, and the directions
conjugate, $h^+=h^-=h$, with $h$ symmetric, positive definite and bounded
with its inverse. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item with a relaxation $\mu>0$, the LATIN iterates converge to the solution,
  in statics and in dynamics;
  \item without relaxation, $\mu=0$, the iterates converge in dynamics under an
  additional condition; in statics, under a stronger monotonicity assumption,
  only the averages $\tfrac12(s_k+s_{k+1})$ are shown to converge.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
Figure~\ref{fig:cy-latin} computes these statements on the truss, over two
periods of the ratchetting load with hardening. With $\mu=0$ the indicator
stalls at $3\times10^{-3}$, although the constitutive iterate is exact to
$10^{-12}\varepsilon_Y$: the admissible iterate oscillates around the solution
and only an average converges. With $\mu=0.3$ the indicator falls to
$10^{-12}$ in 126 iterations for $h=1/E$ and in 297 for $h=4/E$. The relaxation
is the averaging of the Krasnosel'ski\u{\i}--Mann iteration of
Appendix~\ref{app:iter}: it turns a map that is only non-expansive into one
whose iterates converge. On this small problem the whole-history
initial-strain iteration (Box~6.2) is faster still, 84 iterations; its
convergence is not guaranteed by any theorem, that of LATIN is.

\paragraph{The third principle: approximations in time.}
\index{LATIN method!radial approximation}\index{proper generalized decomposition}%
The global stage of LATIN is a linear problem on $\Omega\times[0,T]$. Its cost
is what decides the method on large models. Ladev\`eze's third principle
approximates its correction by a short sum of products of functions of space
and of time, the \emph{radial} approximation, now called the proper
generalized decomposition (PGD)~\cite{LadevezeNCSM,NeronLadeveze2010}; for
loads of many cycles, the time functions are computed on a few ``nodal''
cycles and interpolated between them~\cite{CognardLadeveze1993}. Comte et
al.~\cite{ComteEtAl2006} use the elastic direction and a wavelet basis in
time, in which the global stage decouples into one elastic problem per
retained coefficient.

\section{Cyclic loads: the period map}\label{sec:cy-period}
\index{period map}\index{shakedown!period map}%
\subsection{Asymptotic regimes}
A structure under a load of period $T$ reaches one of three asymptotic
regimes~\cite[Sec.~4.2]{Maitournam2012c}.
\index{shakedown}\index{alternating plasticity}\index{ratchetting}%
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Elastic shakedown} (adaptation): the dissipated energy stays
  bounded, the plastic strain and the internal variables tend to fixed
  fields, and the asymptotic response is elastic: $\sig=\sig^{el}(t)+\vrho_\infty$.
  \item \emph{Alternating plasticity} (accommodation, plastic shakedown): the
  plastic strain becomes periodic, with a non-zero dissipation in every
  cycle; failure comes by low-cycle fatigue.
  \item \emph{Ratchetting}: the plastic strain grows from cycle to cycle;
  failure comes by excessive deformation.
\end{itemize}
For an elastic--perfectly plastic structure, Halphen~\cite{Halphen1978} proved
that a periodic solution in stress exists and that every solution tends to one.
For generalized standard materials with a quadratic hardening energy,
periodic solutions exist in generalized stress and in internal-variable rates,
every solution tends to one of them, and two asymptotic solutions differ by a
constant field. With linear kinematic hardening, $H>0$, stresses and strains
tend to a periodic solution, and ratchetting is
excluded~\cite[Sec.~4.2]{Maitournam2012c}. The three loadings of the truss and
the Bree diagram show the three regimes with perfect plasticity; with $H>0$
their ratchetting cases shake down.

\subsection{The period map}
\begin{definition}\label{def:cy-period}
The \emph{period map} $\Pi$ takes the internal variables at the beginning of a
cycle to their values at its end, $\Pi:\vz(t_0)\mapsto\vz(t_0+T)$, the cycle
being computed under the periodic load. The periodic states are the fixed
points of $\Pi$; the cycle-by-cycle computation is the Picard iteration
$\vz^{(k+1)}=\Pi\,\vz^{(k)}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[The period map does not expand]\label{prop:cy-nonexp}
For two initial states $\vz$, $\vz'$,
\[
  \abs{\Pi\vz-\Pi\vz'}\le\abs{\vz-\vz'},\qquad
  \abs{\vz}^2=\int_\Omega\bigl(\epsp:\Zop\epsp+\valpha\cdot\bbD\valpha\bigr)\dd x .
\]
The quantity $\abs{\cdot}$ is a semi-norm. It vanishes on compatible plastic
strains with $\valpha=\vzero$, and it is a norm on the plastic strains when
the hardening is linear kinematic.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The two solutions are admissible and constitutive. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:cy-signs}, or the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:pl-unique},
$\norm{\Delta\vSig(t)}_E$ does not increase in time. At the beginning and at
the end of the cycle the loads are the same, so that
$\Delta\vSig=(-\Zop\Delta\epsp,-\bbD\Delta\valpha)$ and, since
$\Zop\bbS\Zop=\Zop$, $\norm{\Delta\vSig}_E^2=\abs{\Delta\vz}^2$. For linear
kinematic hardening $\valpha=\epsp$, and $\abs{\vz}^2\ge\int_\Omega\epsp:\bbD\epsp\dd x$.
\end{proof}
Three consequences follow, on the two model problems.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Ratchetting is a drift in the kernel.} Without hardening, the
  semi-norm does not see compatible plastic strains: in the truss the
  increment $(a,-a,0)$ of each cycle, in the Bree vessel the uniform part
  $\langle\varepsilon^p\rangle$. Nothing contracts them, and nothing stops them.
  \item \emph{Periodic states need not be unique.} A non-expansive map may have
  many fixed points. Figure~\ref{fig:cy-nonunique} shows two exact periodic
  states of Bree case~(b) with $H=0.02E$: one reached cycle by cycle from the
  virgin state, one reached by the direct cyclic method. Their stress cycles
  have the same shape and differ by a constant residual stress of
  $0.30\,\sY$ inside the wall. The asymptotic state depends on the history,
  and a direct method selects one periodic state by its own starting point.
  \item \emph{Convergence is slow, but can be accelerated.} When
  $\abs{\cdot}$ is a norm, $\Pi$ is non-expansive in a Hilbert space: an
  orbit is bounded if and only if $\Pi$ has a fixed point~\cite{Pazy1971}, and
  the averaged (Krasnosel'ski\u{\i}--Mann) iteration converges to a fixed point
  (Appendix~\ref{app:iter}). In Bree case~(a) with $H=0.02E$, the
  structure shakes down after 261 cycles. Anderson acceleration of $\Pi$
  (Appendix~\ref{app:iter}), which combines the last five cycles, needs 14
  (Figure~\ref{fig:cy-bree-convergence}). Averaging alone, with $\theta=\tfrac12$,
  is slower than the plain cycles here: its guarantee is not a speed.
\end{itemize}

\section{The direct cyclic and stationary methods}\label{sec:cy-dcm}
\subsection{The direct cyclic method}
\index{direct cyclic method}%
The direct cyclic method is Box~6.2 on one period with the periodic closure
$\vz(t_0)\gets\vz(t_N)$~\cite{AkelNguyen1991,MaoucheMaitournamDangVan1997}.
The global stage solves elastic problems with initial strains at all the
instants of the period, with one factorization of the elastic stiffness; the
local stage sweeps the period at each Gauss point with the return map.
Maitournam, Pommier and Thomas~\cite{MaitournamPommierThomas2002} represent the
periodic fields by truncated Fourier series in time, so that the global stage
handles a few harmonics instead of all the instants; commercial codes offer
the method under the name ``direct cyclic'', with the elastic stiffness as the
iteration matrix. The method reads its own diagnosis:
\begin{itemize}
  \item convergence with $\epsp$ constant in time: elastic shakedown;
  \item convergence with $\epsp$ periodic: alternating plasticity;
  \item no convergence, with a drift of the compatible part of $\epsp$:
  ratchetting.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The computation, in time and through the wall.}
Figure~\ref{fig:cy-bree-process} follows the direct cyclic method and the
cycle-by-cycle computation on Bree case~(a) with hardening. The two
computations build the same kind of profiles: the plastic strain of the outer
half of the wall grows, its inner half receives a uniform plastic strain, the
stress at the hot instant flattens close to the yield stress in the outer half. The
direct cyclic iterates are not cycles, however: they move all the instants at
once, and they reach the profiles through states that no cycle visits. After
400 iterations the plain method still has a residual of
$10^{-4}\varepsilon_Y$ (Figure~\ref{fig:cy-bree-convergence}); with Anderson
acceleration on the whole history (Appendix~\ref{app:iter}) it converges in 16
iterations. Its limit is again a periodic state, different from the
cycle-by-cycle one: they differ mostly by a uniform plastic strain of
$0.050\,\varepsilon_Y$, a vector of the kernel of $\Zop$, and their residual
stresses by $0.7\,\%$ of $\sY$.

\paragraph{Ratchetting seen by the direct cyclic method.}
\index{ratchetting!direct cyclic method}%
Without hardening, the same load ratchets
(Figure~\ref{fig:cy-bree-ratchet}). The direct cyclic iterates do not
converge: the mean plastic strain $\langle\varepsilon^p\rangle$ grows by
$0.060\,\varepsilon_Y$ per iteration, while the residual stress, the
incompatible part, converges to machine precision in 40 iterations. The
method thus finds the stabilized stress cycle of a ratchetting structure,
which is what a fatigue criterion needs, and signals the ratchet by the drift.
The drift per iteration is not the ratchet strain per cycle, which is
$0.32\,\varepsilon_Y$ in the incremental computation: the iterates are not
cycles.

\begin{gbox}{Status of the direct cyclic method}
Convergence of the direct cyclic iteration to a periodic solution is not proved
in general; neither is the convergence of the direct stationary method of the
next section~\cite[Sec.~4.2.2]{DangVanMaitournam1993}. Its fixed points are the
periodic states, which may be non-unique, and its non-convergence is the
numerical signature of ratchetting. Acceleration (Anderson) and averaging are
cheap and should be used.
\end{gbox}

\subsection{Stationary methods}\label{ssec:cy-stat}
\index{stationary method}\index{moving load}%
A load that moves at constant speed $V$ along $\vect e_1$ over a long body (a
wheel on a rail, a rolling bearing, a laser or a welding torch) is stationary
in the frame of the load. With $x=X-Vt$, all the fields depend on $x$ only,
and the material derivative becomes a derivative along the streamlines:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cy-stationary}
  \dot{(\cdot)}=-V\,\frac{\partial(\cdot)}{\partial x} .
\end{equation}
In this frame the material flows towards decreasing $x$: the initial state is
the state far ahead of the load (upstream), at $x=+\infty$, and the final state
the state far behind it (downstream), at $x=-\infty$. In quasi-statics the solution does not depend
on $V$: time is replaced by the position along the streamline. Nguyen and
Rahimian~\cite{NguyenRahimian1981} introduced this formulation for a crack
moving in an elastoplastic medium; Dang Van and
Maitournam~\cite{DangVanMaitournam1993} wrote it for rolling and sliding
contact and proved that the stresses of the stationary problem are unique.

\begin{gbox}{Box 6.4\quad Stationary methods}
\index{stationary method!Box 6.4}%
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Elastic solution} in the frame of the load, $\sig^{el}(x)$;
  $\epsp=\epsp(+\infty)$ everywhere.
  \item \textbf{Local stage.} On each streamline, from upstream to downstream,
  integrate the flow rule with the return map, the position $x$ playing the
  role of time.
  \item \textbf{Global stage.} Solve the elastic problem with the initial strain
  $\epsp(x)$, with the elastic stiffness factorized once.
  \item \textbf{Test.} Repeat 2--3 until the stresses are plastically admissible:
  this is one pass of the load (pass-by-pass stationary method, PPSM).
  \item \textbf{Repeated passes.} Either repeat 1--4 with the downstream state as
  the new upstream state, or impose $\epsp(-\infty)=\epsp(+\infty)$ on every
  streamline as the closure (direct stationary method, DSM).
\end{enumerate}
\end{gbox}
The stationary methods are the direct cyclic method with the period replaced
by one passage of the load and the time replaced by the position: same
global stage, same local stage, a different closure. They are implemented in
the finite element code Cast3M as the procedure \texttt{@STATIO}, which
requires a mesh made of columns along the direction of motion; its example is
a rail under a moving Hertzian pressure, with the stabilized stresses fed to a
multiaxial fatigue criterion.

\section{Zarka's simplified analysis}\label{sec:cy-zarka}
\index{Zarka's method}\index{transformed parameter}%
\subsection{The transformed parameter}
With linear kinematic hardening, the yield condition of a fibre is
$|\sigma-H\varepsilon^p|\le\sY$. Write the stress with Melan,
$\sigma=\sigma^{el}+\rho$, and define the \emph{transformed internal parameter}
of Zarka~\cite{ZarkaCasier1981,ZarkaEtAl1990}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cy-zarka-Y}
  Y=H\varepsilon^p-\rho=(H\,\mathbb I+\Zop)\,\varepsilon^p .
\end{equation}
The yield condition becomes $|\sigma^{el}(t)-Y|\le\sY$: in the space of $Y$,
the elastic domain is an interval (in three dimensions, a von Mises ball, with
$Y=H\epsp-\dev\vrho$) centred on the \emph{elastic} stress, which is known in
advance. Since $H\,\mathbb I+\Zop$ is positive definite, every field $Y$ is
reached by one plastic strain field and only one, given by a linear elastic
problem with modified moduli. Self-equilibrium is no longer a constraint on
$Y$: the problem has become local.

\begin{theorem}[Elastic shakedown with linear kinematic hardening~\cite{MandelHalphenZarka1977}]\label{thm:cy-zarka}
If at every point there is a $Y^\ast$ inside all the elastic domains of the
cycle,
\[
  |\sigma^{el}(x,t)-Y^\ast(x)|\le\sY'<\sY\qquad\forall t\in[0,T],
\]
the structure shakes down elastically, whatever its initial state. The
limit transformed parameter lies, at every point, in the intersection of the
elastic domains of the cycle.
\end{theorem}
The condition is local: in a fibre it reads
$\max_t\sigma^{el}-\min_t\sigma^{el}<2\sY$; in three dimensions it asks that
the path of $\dev\sig^{el}(x,t)$ fit in a ball of radius
$\sqrt{2/3}\,\sY$, and the radius of the smallest such ball is the
\emph{elastic fluctuation} of the point~\cite[Sec.~4.4.3]{Maitournam2012c}.
The shakedown load of a structure with linear kinematic hardening is thus
computed from its elastic solution alone.

\subsection{The construction}
Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-zarka} tells where the limit is, not which point of the
intersection the history selects. Zarka's construction picks one by a
projection.
\begin{gbox}{Box 6.5\quad Zarka's construction (elastic shakedown)}
\index{Zarka's method!Box 6.5}%
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Elastic solution} over the cycle, $\sig^{el}(x,t)$.
  \item \textbf{Admissible set} of $Y$ at each point: the intersection of the
  elastic domains centred on $\sig^{el}(x,t)$; in a fibre, the interval
  $[\max_t\sigma^{el}-\sY,\ \min_t\sigma^{el}+\sY]$.
  \item \textbf{First half-cycle} computed incrementally (or estimated): $Y_1$.
  \item \textbf{Projection} of $Y_1$ onto the admissible set at each point: $Y_\infty$.
  \item \textbf{Plastic strain and residual stress}: solve
  $(H\,\mathbb I+\Zop)\,\epsp_\infty=Y_\infty$ (one linear problem),
  $\vrho_\infty=-\Zop\epsp_\infty$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{gbox}
Figure~\ref{fig:cy-zarka} applies Box~6.5 to Bree case~(a) with $H=0.02E$, the
case where the cycle-by-cycle computation needs 261 cycles. In the inner half of
the wall, $Y_1$ lies below the admissible band and is projected onto its lower
edge; in the outer half it is already inside. The resulting plastic strain is
an exact periodic state and coincides with the cycle-by-cycle limit to
$10^{-8}\varepsilon_Y$, for the cost of half a cycle and one linear problem. The
limit of the accelerated direct cyclic method lies in the same band, at
another point. For alternating plasticity, Zarka's method gives the mean
value and bounds of the range of $Y$, not the cycle itself; Amiable et
al.~\cite{AmiableEtAl2006} compare it with full computations of a thermal
fatigue test. Inglebert and Frelat~\cite{InglebertFrelat1989} describe an
iterative version; Cast3M implements it as the procedure \texttt{@ZACPLUS}.

\framepart{Summary}
\begin{gbox*}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Two model problems}: the three-bar truss (one redundancy,
  self-stresses $\gamma(1,1,-\sqrt2)$, limit load $(1+\sqrt2)N_0$) and the Bree
  vessel (residual stress $-\tilde E(\varepsilon^p-\langle\varepsilon^p\rangle)$,
  Bree diagram). Both show elastic shakedown, alternating plasticity and
  ratchetting.
  \item \textbf{Two groups of equations}: admissible histories $\Ad$ (linear,
  global, with a closure condition) and constitutive histories $\Gcon$
  (nonlinear, local); the solution is $\Ad\cap\Gcon$. On $\Ad$ the dissipation
  of a difference is non-positive, on $\Gcon$ non-negative.
  \item \textbf{Melan}: $\eps=\eps^{el}+\Pop\epsp$, $\sig=\sig^{el}-\Zop\epsp$;
  compatible plastic strains, the kernel of $\Zop$, give no residual stress and
  are the direction of ratchetting.
  \item \textbf{Two orders}: incremental (for $n$, for $k$) inherits the
  guarantees of each step; the iteration on the whole history (for $k$, for
  $n$) has none in general and carries errors forward without damping. It
  pays when plasticity is confined, when many transient cycles are avoided,
  and when factorizations are expensive.
  \item \textbf{Search directions}: the return map is the ``vertical'' local
  direction; the global direction gives Newton (tangent) or the initial-strain
  method (elastic, factor $E/(E+H)$ for a fibre). LATIN uses conjugate
  directions on the rates over $[0,T]$ and converges for monotone materials,
  in statics only with a relaxation $\mu>0$.
  \item \textbf{Period map}: $\Pi:\vz(0)\mapsto\vz(T)$ does not expand the
  energy semi-norm; ratchetting is a drift in its kernel; periodic states
  may be non-unique; Anderson acceleration cuts hundreds of cycles to tens.
  \item \textbf{Direct cyclic and stationary methods}: elastic global stage,
  return-map sweep, periodic or upstream--downstream closure. Convergence
  means shakedown, a drift means ratchetting; convergence is not proved in
  general.
  \item \textbf{Zarka}: with linear kinematic hardening, $Y=H\epsp-\vrho$ turns
  shakedown into a local condition on the elastic solution, and the shakedown
  state into a projection plus one linear problem.
\end{itemize}
\end{gbox*}

\exercise{Regimes of the three-bar truss}\label{exo:cy-q-truss}
\index{three-bar truss!exercise}%
Elastic--perfectly plastic bars, $N_0=S\sY$. Using~\eqref{eq:cy-truss-el} and
Melan's theorem, find the regime under (a) $Q_1=1.1N_0(1-\cos t)$, $Q_2=0$;
(b) $Q_1=1.9N_0\sin t$, $Q_2=0$. (c) Under $Q_2=N_0$ and $Q_1=1.2N_0\sin t$,
show that the plastic elongation $(a,-a,0)$ is compatible, and explain why it
can grow without bound.
\begin{solution}
(a) $N^{el}_3=2Q_1/(2+\sqrt2)$ varies in $[0,\,1.289N_0]$ and $N^{el}_{1,2}$ in
$[0,\,0.644N_0]$. The constant residual field $\gamma(1,1,-\sqrt2)$ with
$\gamma=0.25N_0$ gives $N_3\in[-0.354,\,0.935]N_0$ and
$N_{1,2}\in[0.25,\,0.894]N_0$, inside $[-N_0,N_0]$: elastic shakedown (Melan).
Bar~3 yields in the first cycle since $\max N^{el}_3>N_0$. (b) The range of
$N^{el}_3$ is $4\times1.9/(2+\sqrt2)\,N_0=2.23N_0>2N_0$; no residual field can
bring it into $[-N_0,N_0]$: bar~3 yields in every cycle, alternating
plasticity. (c) $\delta^p_1+\delta^p_2-\sqrt2\,\delta^p_3=0$, so
by~\eqref{eq:cy-truss-res} the residual forces vanish: the increment is the
elongation of a vertical displacement of $O$. Nothing in the stresses
resists it; the computation gives $a=0.4\,\delta_Y$ per cycle.
\end{solution}

\exercise{Melan decomposition of the Bree vessel}\label{exo:cy-q-bree}
\index{Bree problem!exercise}%
From~\eqref{eq:cy-bree}, derive~\eqref{eq:cy-bree-melan}. Show that
$\Pop\varepsilon^p=\langle\varepsilon^p\rangle$ and that the kernel of $\Zop$ is
the set of uniform plastic strains. What does a uniform plastic strain mean
for the vessel?
\begin{solution}
Take the mean of~\eqref{eq:cy-bree} through the wall: $\langle x\rangle=0$, so
$\sigma_P=\tilde E(\varepsilon-\langle\varepsilon^p\rangle)$ and
$\varepsilon=\sigma_P/\tilde E+\langle\varepsilon^p\rangle$; substituting,
$\sigma=\sigma^{el}-\tilde E(\varepsilon^p-\langle\varepsilon^p\rangle)$. The
compatible strains are the uniform ones ($\mat{B}$ is a column of ones), and
$\Pop$ is the orthogonal projection on them, the mean. $\Zop\varepsilon^p=0$
if and only if $\varepsilon^p$ equals its mean. A uniform hoop plastic strain
is a permanent increase of the radius: the vessel swells without stress. It
is the ratchet strain of the Bree problem.
\end{solution}

\exercise{Contraction factor in the truss}\label{exo:cy-q-factor}
\index{initial-strain method!exercise}%
In the truss loaded by $Q_1$ beyond first yield, bar~3 is plastic with
hardening $H$ and bars~1, 2 are elastic. Show that the initial-strain
iteration multiplies the error on the displacement $u_1$ of $O$ by
$\kappa=1-K_t/K$, where $K=(1+1/\sqrt2)ES/l$ and $K_t=(E_t+E/\sqrt2)S/l$ are
the elastic and tangent stiffnesses of the truss in the direction $\vect e_1$.
Compare with Proposition~\ref{prop:cy-factor} and discuss $H=0$.
\begin{solution}
By symmetry $u_2=0$. Bar~3 has the strain $u_1/l$; bars~1 and~2 have
$u_1/(2l)$ each and the projection factor $1/\sqrt2$ on $\vect e_1$. The
stiffness of the truss in $\vect e_1$ is $(E+2\cdot\tfrac12\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}E)S/l
=(1+1/\sqrt2)ES/l$ elastically, and the same with $E_t$ for bar~3 on its plastic
branch. The residual is affine in $u_1$ with slope $K_t$, and the iteration
divides it by $K$: $\kappa=1-K_t/K$. With $H=0$, $E_t=0$ and
$\kappa=1-\frac{1/\sqrt2}{1+1/\sqrt2}=\frac{\sqrt2}{1+\sqrt2}\approx0.59$: the
iteration still converges, because the elastic bars confine bar~3. For a
single fibre $K_t/K=E_t/E$ and $\kappa=E/(E+H)$, equal to $1$ without
hardening.
\end{solution}

\exercise{Relaxation in LATIN}\label{exo:cy-q-relax}
\index{LATIN method!relaxation (exercise)}%
A linear map $x\mapsto -x$ has the unique fixed point $0$. (a) Show that its
Picard iterates do not converge from $x_0\ne0$, but that their averages
$\tfrac12(x_k+x_{k+1})$ do. (b) Show that the relaxed iteration
$x_{k+1}=(1-\mu)(-x_k)+\mu x_k$ converges for $0<\mu<1$. (c) Relate (a) and (b)
to Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-latin} and Figure~\ref{fig:cy-latin}.
\begin{solution}
(a) $x_k=(-1)^kx_0$ oscillates; $\tfrac12(x_k+x_{k+1})=0$. (b)
$x_{k+1}=(2\mu-1)x_k$ and $|2\mu-1|<1$. (c) Without relaxation, the LATIN
iteration in statics may behave like (a): an iterate oscillates around the
solution, here the admissible one, while an average converges; the
indicator stalls in Figure~\ref{fig:cy-latin}. The relaxation is the
averaging of (b), as in the Krasnosel'ski\u{\i}--Mann iteration of
Appendix~\ref{app:iter}.
\end{solution}

\exercise{Moving frame}\label{exo:cy-q-moving}
\index{stationary method!exercise}%
A field $f(X,t)$ is stationary in the frame of a load moving at speed $V$
along $\vect e_1$: $f(X,t)=g(x)$ with $x=X-Vt$. (a)
Derive~\eqref{eq:cy-stationary} and explain why the initial state is
prescribed at $x=+\infty$. (b) Show that for a rate-independent material the
stationary solution does not depend on $V$.
\begin{solution}
(a) At fixed material point $X$, $\partial f/\partial t=g'(x)\,\partial x/\partial t=-Vg'(x)$.
A material point sees $x$ decrease: it comes from $x=+\infty$, ahead of the
load, where it is still in its initial state, and leaves towards
$x=-\infty$. (b) The flow rule of a
rate-independent material is invariant under a change of time scale: the
rate $-V\partial_x$ multiplies all the rates by the same positive factor, and
the normal cone is a cone. The evolution along a streamline depends on the
position only.
\end{solution}

\exercise{Zarka for one fibre}\label{exo:cy-q-zarka}
\index{Zarka's method!exercise}%
A fibre with linear kinematic hardening has an elastic stress cycling between
$\sigma_{\min}$ and $\sigma_{\max}$. (a) When can it shake down elastically?
(b) After the first half-cycle the transformed parameter is
$Y_1=\sigma_{\max}-\sY$. Where does Zarka's construction place $Y_\infty$?
(c) What happens if $\sigma_{\max}-\sigma_{\min}>2\sY$?
\begin{solution}
(a) If $\sigma_{\max}-\sigma_{\min}\le2\sY$: the interval
$[\sigma_{\max}-\sY,\,\sigma_{\min}+\sY]$ is not empty. (b) $Y_1$ is the lower
end of the interval, already admissible: $Y_\infty=Y_1$; the fibre shakes down
after its first yield in tension. (c) The interval is empty: no fixed $Y$ is
admissible at both extremes; the fibre yields in every cycle (alternating
plasticity), and Zarka's method only bounds the range of $Y$.
\end{solution}

\exercise{Regimes and orders of computation on the truss}\label{exo:cy-truss}
\index{three-bar truss!computation}%
(a) Compute 30 cycles of each loading of Section~\ref{ssec:cy-truss} with
perfect plasticity, and classify the regimes by the drift per cycle and the
range of the plastic strain within a cycle. (b) With $H=0.05E$, compute two
periods of the third loading step by step with Newton's method, step by step
with the initial-strain iteration (Box~6.1), and with the whole-history
iteration (Box~6.2). Count the local evaluations.
\begin{solution}
(a) Loading 1: no drift, no range: elastic shakedown (bar~3 keeps the
plastic strain of its first yield). Loading 2: no drift, a range of

$(+0.4,-0.4,0)\,\varepsilon_Y$ per cycle in bars 1 and 2: ratchetting along the
compatible direction. (b) Newton: 178 local evaluations, 2 or 3 iterations per
step. Initial strain: 982 evaluations, from 2 iterations at the elastic steps
to 46 at the plastic ones. Whole history: 64 iterations of 80 instants, 5120
evaluations. On this small structure the step-by-step methods are cheaper; the
whole-history iteration needs one factorization only.

\exercise{LATIN on the truss: directions and relaxation}\label{exo:cy-latin}
\index{LATIN method!exercise}%
On the window of two periods of the third loading of the truss ($H=0.05E$),
run Box~6.3 with $hE=1$ and $hE=4$, without and with the relaxation $\mu=0.3$.
Compare the error indicator and the error of the constitutive iterate on the
plastic strain with respect to the step-by-step solution, and explain the
case $\mu=0$ with Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-latin}.
\begin{solution}
With $\mu=0.3$ the indicator reaches $10^{-10}$ in 107 iterations ($hE=1$) and
266 ($hE=4$); the plastic strain agrees with the step-by-step solution to

while the constitutive iterate is exact to $6\times10^{-13}\varepsilon_Y$: the
admissible iterate oscillates around the solution, and in statics the theorem
guarantees only the convergence of averages. The relaxation removes the
oscillation (Exercise~\ref{exo:cy-q-relax}).

\exercise{The Bree vessel: period map and direct cyclic method}\label{exo:cy-bree}
\index{Bree problem!computation}%
Take the Bree vessel with $X=0.7$, $Y=1.5$ (100 layers, 40 steps per cycle).
(a) Without hardening, compute 20 cycles; give the drift of the mean plastic
strain and the change of the residual stress per cycle. (b) With $H=0.02E$,
iterate on the period map with Picard (cycle by cycle), averaging
($\theta=\tfrac12$) and Anderson acceleration (Algorithm~\ref{alg:cy-anderson}),
then run the direct cyclic method plain and accelerated. (c) Compare the two
periodic states obtained cycle by cycle and with the accelerated direct cyclic
method.
\begin{solution}
(a) The mean plastic strain grows by $0.317\,\varepsilon_Y$ per cycle and the
residual stress does not change ($10^{-16}\sY$): the ratchet is a drift in the
kernel of $\Zop$. (b) Picard needs 261 cycles to reach $10^{-10}\varepsilon_Y$;
averaging is not converged after 400 ($6\times10^{-8}\varepsilon_Y$); Anderson
needs 14 cycles. The plain direct cyclic method has a residual of
$10^{-4}\varepsilon_Y$ after 400 sweeps; accelerated, it converges in 16. (c)
Both states are exact fixed points of $\Pi$. They differ by a mean plastic
strain of $0.050\,\varepsilon_Y$ (a compatible field) and by residual stresses of
$0.0068\,\sY$: the periodic state is not unique, and each method selects its own.
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Anderson acceleration of a fixed-point map}\label{alg:cy-anderson}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require map $M$ (period map $\Pi$ or one direct cyclic sweep), start $z_0$, depth $m$, tolerance $\epsilon$
\Ensure a fixed point $z^\ast\approx M(z^\ast)$
\For{$k=0,1,2,\dots$}
  \State $g_k\gets M(z_k)$;\ \ $f_k\gets g_k-z_k$ \Comment{one cycle or one sweep}
  \If{$\norm{f_k}_\infty<\epsilon$} \Return $z_k$ \EndIf
  \State $m_k\gets\min(m,k)$;\ \ $\Delta F\gets[f_{i+1}-f_i]_{i=k-m_k}^{k-1}$;\ \ $\Delta G\gets[g_{i+1}-g_i]_{i=k-m_k}^{k-1}$
  \State $\gamma\gets\arg\min_\gamma\norm{f_k-\Delta F\,\gamma}_2$ \Comment{small least squares}
  \State $z_{k+1}\gets g_k-\Delta G\,\gamma$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
\lstinputlisting[firstline=4]{python/examples/ch6/exo_bree_cyclic.py}
\end{solution}

\exercise{Zarka's estimate of the shakedown state}\label{exo:cy-zarka}
\index{Zarka's method!computation}%
Apply Box~6.5 to the Bree vessel with $H=0.02E$ for $(X,Y)=(0.6,1.5)$ and
$(0.7,1.5)$, and compare with the cycle-by-cycle limits.
\begin{solution}
Elastic shakedown is possible at every layer in both cases. For $(0.6,1.5)$ the
structure shakes down in its first cycle and Zarka's estimate coincides with
the limit to $5\times10^{-14}\varepsilon_Y$. For $(0.7,1.5)$ the cycle-by-cycle
computation needs 261 cycles; Zarka's estimate, half a cycle and one linear
problem, coincides with its limit to $5\times10^{-9}\varepsilon_Y$ (the
tolerance of the cycles) and is an exact periodic state
($\abs{\Pi\epsp-\epsp}<10^{-14}\varepsilon_Y$).
